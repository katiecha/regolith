# Regolith — Abstract (Home Page)

## Thesis

Most software today is optimized for speed of production, not clarity of thought.

As a result, we get interfaces that are functional but forgettable, systems that scale but are difficult to reason about, and products that prioritize growth over coherence.

Regolith is an exploration of a different approach:
building software that is not only correct and performant, but *legible* — to users, to developers, and to itself.

This means:
- prioritizing clarity over cleverness
- designing systems that reveal their structure instead of hiding it
- treating interfaces as arguments, not just surfaces

The goal is not minimalism for its own sake, but precision:
removing everything that does not contribute to understanding.

---

## Why This Matters

Software increasingly mediates how we think, work, and make decisions.

When systems are opaque:
- users lose trust
- developers lose velocity
- complexity compounds silently

Legible systems, by contrast:
- scale more sustainably
- are easier to debug and extend
- create better user experiences through clarity

Regolith is a small step toward that direction.
